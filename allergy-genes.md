# Allergy-Related Genes to Investigate

A reference for exploring genome report results related to allergic disease susceptibility.

## Skin Barrier

| Gene | Full Name | Relevance |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **FLG** | Filaggrin | One of the strongest genetic risk factors for allergies. Loss-of-function variants impair the skin barrier, leading to increased allergen penetration. Strongly associated with eczema (atopic dermatitis) and subsequent development of food allergies and asthma ("atopic march"). |

## IgE Pathway (Antibody Production)

| Gene | Full Name | Relevance |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **FCER1A** | High-affinity IgE receptor, alpha subunit | Encodes the alpha chain of the receptor that binds IgE on mast cells and basophils. Variants affect IgE levels and allergic sensitization. |
| **MS4A2** (FCER1B) | High-affinity IgE receptor, beta subunit | Amplifies IgE receptor signaling. Variants linked to elevated total serum IgE and atopy. |
| **STAT6** | Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 6 | Downstream signaling molecule for IL-4/IL-13. Drives IgE class switching in B cells. Variants associated with elevated IgE and asthma. |

## Cytokines (Immune Signaling)

| Gene | Full Name | Relevance |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **IL-4** | Interleukin-4 | Key Th2 cytokine that drives IgE production. Variants in the promoter region associated with atopy and asthma. |
| **IL-13** | Interleukin-13 | Close neighbor of IL-4 on chromosome 5q31. Promotes mucus production, airway hyperresponsiveness, and IgE synthesis. |
| **IL-33** | Interleukin-33 | "Alarmin" cytokine released by epithelial cells upon damage. Activates mast cells and Th2 responses. GWAS-identified risk locus for asthma and allergic rhinitis. |
| **IL4R** (IL4RA) | Interleukin-4 Receptor Alpha | Shared receptor subunit for both IL-4 and IL-13 signaling. Gain-of-function variants (e.g., Q576R) associated with increased allergic disease risk. |
| **TSLP** | Thymic Stromal Lymphopoietin | Epithelial-derived cytokine that initiates Th2 allergic inflammation. Variants associated with asthma, eczema, and allergic rhinitis. A drug target (tezepelumab). |

## Immune Regulation

| Gene | Full Name | Relevance |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **HLA region** | Human Leukocyte Antigen | MHC class II genes (especially HLA-DQ, HLA-DR) determine which allergen peptides are presented to T cells. Specific alleles associated with peanut allergy, cedar pollen allergy, etc. |
| **FOXP3** | Forkhead Box P3 | Master regulator of regulatory T cells (Tregs). Tregs suppress allergic responses. Rare mutations cause IPEX syndrome with severe multi-organ allergy. |
| **IL-10** | Interleukin-10 | Anti-inflammatory cytokine. Important for immune tolerance to allergens. Low-producer variants may impair tolerance. |

## GWAS-Identified Loci

| Gene / Locus | Relevance |
|--------------|-----------|
| **C11orf30 / EMSY** | Consistently identified in GWAS for multiple allergic diseases (asthma, eczema, rhinitis, food allergy). Likely involved in epigenetic regulation of immune genes. |
| **TNFSF4** (OX40L) | Co-stimulatory molecule involved in T cell activation. Variants associated with allergic rhinitis and asthma. |
| **WDR36** | Associated with allergic rhinitis in large GWAS studies. |
| **SLC25A46** | Mitochondrial protein; associated with allergic rhinitis in GWAS. Mechanism not fully understood. |

## Metabolism / Drug Response

| Gene | Full Name | Relevance |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **HRH1** | Histamine Receptor H1 | The receptor that antihistamines block. Variants may influence antihistamine response. |
| **ALOX5** | Arachidonate 5-Lipoxygenase | Produces leukotrienes, major mediators of allergic inflammation. Promoter variants affect response to leukotriene inhibitors (montelukast). |
| **ADRB2** | Beta-2 Adrenergic Receptor | Target of rescue inhalers (albuterol). Variants (e.g., Arg16Gly) associated with differing bronchodilator response. |

## How to Use This List

1. **Search your genome report** for these gene names or their rs-number SNP identifiers
2. **Look for flagged variants** — most consumer reports highlight clinically relevant or well-studied SNPs
3. **Cross-reference with databases**:
   - [ClinVar](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/) — clinical significance of variants
   - [SNPedia](https://www.snpedia.com/) — community-curated SNP info
   - [GWAS Catalog](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/) — published genome-wide associations
4. **Talk to a genetic counselor** for clinical interpretation of any pathogenic or likely-pathogenic variants

> **Note:** Having a risk variant does not mean you will develop a condition. Allergic diseases are polygenic (many genes contribute small effects) and heavily influenced by environment (microbiome, diet, exposures, geography).
